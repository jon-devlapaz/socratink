# Sal Khan: decisions and action record

**Research date:** 2026-08-08  
**Scope:** consequential choices made by Khan or clearly attributable to his leadership at Khan Academy. This is not a claim that every organization-level action was his alone.  
**Evidence key:** **[F]** independently/officially documented action or outcome; **[S]** Khan's stated rationale; **[I]** analyst inference. **Reliability:** High = primary record or strong direct reporting; Medium = official organizational account of results; Low = unverified anecdote (not used for substantive claims).

## Decision map at a glance

| Period | Decision | Recurrent logic | Result / learning signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2004-09 | Begin remote tutoring, publish short handwritten videos, then leave finance | Make explanation asynchronously reusable; follow evidence of learner pull | A side project became Khan Academy; Khan went full-time in fall 2009. |
| 2008-10 | Incorporate as a nonprofit and accept philanthropic, not VC, capital | Protect permanent universal/free access from future owners and profit incentives | Mission durability and volunteer/donor attraction; also recurring fundraising dependence. |
| 2010-12 | Move from a video library to practice, mastery data, and teacher tools through school pilots | Observe real classrooms; use mastery data to let teachers redeploy time | Los Altos expansion followed; Khan explicitly acknowledged the initial test was not controlled research. |
| 2014 onward | Build a physical lab school and later adjacent nonprofits | Test mastery/personalized education in a whole-school setting, not only software | An ongoing laboratory, but a small selective independent-school setting limits generalization. |
| 2015-24 | Partner with large institutions and districts while keeping core classroom tools free; sell implementation support | Scale distribution through systems while preserving free learner access | District program became a major delivery channel; paid services create a tension with the simple “free” public narrative. |
| 2022-26 | Partner early with OpenAI and launch Khanmigo cautiously, then redesign after low voluntary use | Treat AI as Socratic support and teacher amplification; instrument and iterate | Strong early publicity, but Khan later called use a “non-event” for many students; product shifted to in-workflow proactive help and explicit learning metrics. |

## 1. Leave a finance career for the learning project (2005-2009)

**Context and action — [F, High].** Khan began tutoring a cousin remotely in 2004/05, started posting explanations publicly, continued while working in finance, and quit his hedge-fund job in the fall of 2009 to pursue the project full-time. Khan Academy's own history records the timing and its nonprofit status. [Khan Academy history](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202483180-What-is-the-history-of-Khan-Academy)

**Alternatives/constraints.** Continuing as a hobby preserved income and reduced personal risk; a conventional tutoring business could have monetized the growing demand. The project instead required content production, engineering, and operations before a proved revenue model.

**Decision logic — [S, High].** The original behavior reveals the key test: a single explanation can be replayed, paused, and reused by many learners, while one-to-one tutoring cannot scale. In later accounts, Khan describes moving toward a mission institution rather than optimizing personal upside; see his explanation of the nonprofit choice below.

**Outcome and alignment.** **[F, High]** He remained the founder/CEO and still makes videos, according to Khan Academy's leadership page. [Leadership bio](https://www.khanacademy.org/about/our-board) **[I, Medium]** This is a strong words/actions alignment: he accepted the highest personal-risk version of the claim that access and learning mattered more than a finance career. It does not prove that every later product choice follows from that same priority.

## 2. Choose a nonprofit rather than a venture-backed education company (2008 onward)

**Action — [F, High].** Khan Academy was established as a 501(c)(3), a structure it continues to use. It later accepted grants and donations, including Google's $2m 2010 Project 10^100 grant and a disclosed $5m Musk Foundation gift in 2021. [Nonprofit explanation](https://blog.khanacademy.org/did-you-know-khan-academy-is-a-nonprofit/) · [Google announcement](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/company-announcements/project-10100-winners-announced/) · [Khan's Musk thank-you](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=id_MB2RClG4)

**Alternatives/constraints.** Khan says venture capitalists were interested and that the organization resembled a natural Silicon Valley for-profit: rapid growth, internet distribution, and millions of users. A for-profit could use equity compensation and conventional investor capital; it would also introduce investors, acquisition/IPO pathways, and pressure around monetization.

**Decision logic — [S, High].** In a direct Harvard Business Review interview, Khan says he modeled the two “home run” outcomes. The for-profit one could reach 100m or 1bn users and reward founders/investors, but he worried that outside ownership would not preserve universal access over generations. He chose the nonprofit as the more credible durable institution, despite lower compensation flexibility. [HBR interview](https://hbr.org/podcast/2014/01/salman-khan-on-the-online-lear)

**Outcome/evidence — [F, Medium-High].** The organization has sustained a free core service and cites volunteer participation and mission-driven hiring as benefits; the latter are Khan's claims, not independently causal proof. The current donation page confirms tax-deductible nonprofit financing. [Donation page](https://donate.khanacademy.org/campaign/737457/donate)

**Tension / possible contradiction.** **[I, Medium]** “Free, world-class education” is an organizational north star, but a donor-funded organization is still exposed to funder concentration and must develop paid offerings (e.g., district implementation and individual Khanmigo access). That is a structural trade-off rather than evidence of bad faith. In 2023, Khan explicitly asked U.S. adults for $4/month or $44/year to access Khanmigo while characterizing payments as support for the nonprofit mission. [Khan's 2023 AI launch note](https://blog.khanacademy.org/harnessing-ai-so-that-all-students-benefit-a-nonprofit-approach-for-equal-access/)

## 3. Pivot from “videos on YouTube” to mastery practice and classroom implementation (2010-12)

**Action — [F, High].** Khan Academy piloted in Los Altos schools in 2010-11, observed student/teacher use, and built a knowledge map, teacher dashboards, badges, and exercises in response. Los Altos then expanded district-wide to 40+ additional classrooms; Khan Academy chose to study additional charter, independent, low-income, and special-needs contexts. [Khan Academy's contemporaneous account](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202260264-How-is-Khan-Academy-effective-and-different-from-other-resources)

**Alternatives/constraints.** The initial organization was tiny (its own account says five people for most of the year); it could have kept scaling the content library, or asserted efficacy from enthusiastic testimonials. Classroom use required difficult product work and an implementation model that treated teachers as active users, not merely content consumers.

**Decision logic — [S, High].** Khan Academy says students should work toward mastery at their own pace and teachers should use the resulting time/data for projects, peer tutoring, discussion, and individual help—an explicit rejection of treating the videos as a complete school replacement. [Same primary account](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202260264-How-is-Khan-Academy-effective-and-different-from-other-resources)

**Outcome and caveat.** **[F, High]** The pilot produced expansion and concrete features, but the organization openly states it did *not* conduct a controlled research study and that the product changed markedly during the year. This is a useful pattern: Khan acts on field signal first, while retaining (at least in this account) a boundary around what the evidence can establish. Later reporting says the district program supplies dashboards, professional learning, and year-long implementation support rather than merely licenses. [2024 annual report](https://2023-2024.annualreport.khanacademy.org/)

**Alignment.** **[I, High]** The action matches the repeated claim that software should amplify teachers. It also reveals a practical bias toward prototypes and observed usage over waiting for a fully controlled proof.

## 4. Take mastery learning from product thesis to whole-school experiment (Khan Lab School, 2014 onward)

**Action — [F, High].** Khan founded Khan Lab School as a nonprofit, mastery-based independent school and later founded Schoolhouse.world and Khan World School as adjacent nonprofits. The current Khan Academy leadership description attributes all three to him. [Leadership bio](https://www.khanacademy.org/about/our-board) The school's 2025-26 profile identifies it as a non-profit mastery-based independent school and notes a 2024-25 program ending. [KLS profile](https://khanlabschool.org/files/galleries/KLS_SchoolProfile_2025-26.pdf)

**Alternatives/constraints.** He could have remained a software/content provider, leaving pedagogy to school partners. A physical school supplies richer feedback about age-mixing, pacing, assessment, social development, and teacher practice, but it is costly and hard to treat as representative of public systems.

**Decision logic — [S/organizational record, Medium].** Khan's published model argues that age-graded pacing creates “gaps,” while mastery lets pupils spend needed time and gives teachers scope for higher-value work. The school therefore functions as an applied test bed for the same philosophy. [Mastery rationale](https://blog.khanacademy.org/survey-shows-teachers-favor-mastery-learning-to-address-pandemic-related-learning-gaps/)

**Outcome/limits.** **[F, Medium]** The school continues to publish a mastery-oriented curriculum and profile. **[I, High]** Its endurance is evidence of commitment, not proof that it scales: an independent lab environment has selection, staffing, funding, and governance conditions unlike a typical public district. This is a central generalization risk in Khan's reasoning.

## 5. Partner with institutional distributors without abandoning the free core (2015-2025)

**Actions — [F, High].** Khan Academy became the College Board's official SAT practice partner and built district partnerships that pair product access with rostering, dashboards, professional learning, and implementation support. Its current district page says classroom tools are free but the partnership includes paid services; the SY24-25 annual report describes the program's expanded district support. [District-partnership explanation](https://blog.khanacademy.org/becoming-a-khan-academy-districts-partner/) · [SY24-25 annual report](https://annualreport.khanacademy.org/)

**Alternatives/constraints.** A pure consumer model retains simplicity but does not supply districts with integration, data governance, training, and accountability. A full enterprise pivot risks moving attention toward customers able to pay.

**Apparent decision logic — [S/F, Medium-High].** Khan's longstanding theory is that universal content plus teacher context produces more learning than isolated screen time. Partnerships convert that theory into a distribution/implementation mechanism. The 2023 AI launch stated that 500+ schools/districts were partnering (up from nine pre-pandemic) and framed classroom integration as necessary because tutoring disconnected from classwork is less effective. [Khan's signed launch note](https://blog.khanacademy.org/harnessing-ai-so-that-all-students-benefit-a-nonprofit-approach-for-equal-access/)

**Outcome and tension.** **[F, Medium]** This model reached 770,000 U.S. district-partnership students with Khanmigo in SY24-25, according to the annual report. **[I, Medium]** It is a mission-compatible cross-subsidy strategy, but it creates two audiences with different service levels and may pull product priorities toward district buyers. It should be described as a deliberate compromise, not as identical to completely free access.

## 6. Early OpenAI partnership and a deliberately limited Khanmigo launch (2022-23)

**Action — [F, High].** OpenAI leaders contacted Khan in summer 2022 and provided early GPT-4 access; Khan Academy built Khanmigo and initially restricted it to a small Khan Labs pilot of teachers, students, and donors. The product was designed to ask questions rather than hand over answers, and to operate inside class assignments. [Chalkbeat interview/report](https://cbnewsletters.chalkbeat.org/p/sal-khan-once-said-ai-would-revolutionize-schools-now-he-sees-its-limits) · [Khan's signed 2023 announcement](https://blog.khanacademy.org/harnessing-ai-so-that-all-students-benefit-a-nonprofit-approach-for-equal-access/)

**Alternatives/constraints.** He could have rejected generative AI because of hallucinations, cheating, safeguarding, and immature math reliability; or released a general-purpose chatbot immediately to gain adoption. GPT-4's capability and cost were moving quickly, and schools were reacting with bans and concern.

**Decision logic — [S, High].** Khan called this a responsibility to “work deeply” with the technology, but proposed limited access, opt-in district pilots, classwork integration, and a Socratic tutor rather than answer generator. He explicitly warned that even the newest AI could err in math. The rationale combines technological optimism with a theory of *productive struggle*.

**Outcome and alignment.** **[F, High]** The small-pilot promise was substantively carried out through staged access. Current safeguards include risk disclosures, prompt narrowing/fine-tuning, moderation, red teaming, daily usage limits, parent/teacher visibility, under-18 access restrictions, and adult alerts on flagged interactions. [Responsible-AI policy](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/13965308352781-What-is-Khan-Academy-s-approach-to-responsible-AI-development)

**Failure/controversy.** **[F, High]** The safety policy expressly acknowledges factual, mathematical, and harmful-content risks; outside testing has also found errors in early versions. Axios reported Khan's 2023 claim that custom work beyond raw GPT-4 aimed to avoid math errors, while also noting the limited release. [Axios, April 2023](https://www.axios.com/2023/04/07/sal-khan-chatgpt-gpt4-tutor) **[I, Medium]** The tension is real: Khan presented unusually transformative potential while relying on a model family whose errors required continuous guardrails. His public documentation is relatively candid about the risk, but it cannot by itself validate safety or learning impact.

## 7. Reverse the “passive available tutor” design after usage disappointment (2025-26)

**Problem discovered — [F, High].** In an April 2026 interview, Khan said Khanmigo had been a “non-event” for many students because they did not use it much; he compared it to an adult silently waiting at the back of a classroom. He now calls AI “part of the solution,” not the whole solution. [Chalkbeat, Apr. 9 2026](https://cbnewsletters.chalkbeat.org/p/sal-khan-once-said-ai-would-revolutionize-schools-now-he-sees-its-limits)

**Action/reversal — [F, High].** Khan Academy reported that only about 15% of students with access were engaging with Khanmigo, despite 108m+ total interactions since the 2023 launch. It changed the experience from waiting for an ideal question to visible, assignment-embedded, context-aware help: different support before/after an attempt, more information about mastery/prerequisites, and simpler UX. It piloted this with select districts for wider summer-2026 rollout. [Khan Academy, Apr. 28 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/learning-in-the-open-what-ai-is-and-isnt-changing/)

**New decision rule — [F, High].** From Oct. 2025-Apr. 2026 the team tested changes against latency, **next-item correctness** (independent transfer), and cognitive engagement; it also used answer-giving, math-error, and thread metrics as guardrails. It reports a six-percentage-point improvement in the relevant tested measure, while saying no single change was a dramatic leap. [Khan Academy research update](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)

**Interpretation.** **[I, High]** This is Khan's clearest documented post-hoc correction: move from a high-level “AI tutor for every learner” proposition toward behaviorally integrated support measured by whether the learner can do the next problem unaided. The correction aligns with his prior mastery/teacher-amplification thesis, but materially tempers his earlier revolution rhetoric. It also shows a preference for product telemetry and real classroom observation over defending an initial design.

## 8. Management and governance signals

**Continuity — [F, High].** Khan remains founder and CEO, with an executive team and board rather than a founder-only operation. The official leadership page gives him responsibility for vision/direction while identifying a senior team. [Leadership](https://www.khanacademy.org/about/our-board)

**External role — [F, High].** In October 2025, TED named Khan its “vision steward” and board member while appointing Logan McClure Davda as CEO. This is a division of public/idea stewardship from operating management at TED; it is not evidence that Khan left Khan Academy. [TED announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ted-announces-new-leadership-for-its-next-chapter-sal-khan-of-khan-academy-joins-ted-board-as-vision-steward-logan-mcclure-davda-named-ceo-302585127.html)

**Cautious inference — [I, Medium].** Across Khan Academy, his action record is founder-led vision plus mission-constrained institutions and partnerships, not evidence of a recurring pattern of abrupt executive reversals. Public sources reviewed here did not substantiate a specific major management-change or layoff decision attributable to Khan; it should not be invented into the persona.

## Cross-decision patterns for synthesis

1. **Mission durability before ownership upside.** The nonprofit choice, donations, and free core are best read as a long-horizon institutional bet, not anti-market absolutism. He accepts paid support/services when they finance reach.
2. **Scale the *explanation*, then scale the *feedback loop*.** He repeatedly turns a scarce human input into reusable infrastructure: video, exercises/data, teacher dashboards, district implementation, and now AI assistance.
3. **Teachers are an amplifier, not a residual.** His stated model uses technology to free teachers for diagnosis, discussion, projects, and relationships. Treat any claim that he simply wants to replace teachers as criticism to investigate, not a fair summary of his stated design.
4. **Pilot in the real environment; revise openly.** Los Altos generated product changes before controlled proof; Khanmigo's low adoption led to a workflow redesign and measurable guardrails. Strength: adaptability. Risk: early enthusiasm can outrun causal evidence.
5. **Mastery means independent transfer, not activity.** The 2026 “next-item correctness” choice operationalizes the older mastery premise and is more stringent than measuring clicks or chatbot conversations.
6. **Optimism bounded by explicit caveats, but not always by rhetoric.** He launched AI early and promoted its potential while simultaneously documenting fallibility, limited pilots, and safeguards. The gap between transformative framing and modest adoption/effect evidence is an important internal tension, not a reason to discard either record.

## Evidence gaps / honest boundaries

- Direct, independently audited causal evidence for every claimed learning gain was not established in this pass; treat Khan Academy annual-report outcomes as organizational reporting unless a linked study is evaluated.
- Public material does not let us reconstruct private board debates, declined offers, personnel decisions, or every funding negotiation. Do not narrate them as fact.
- Khan Lab School is meaningful as a design experiment but insufficient evidence on its own for district-scale effectiveness.
- 2026 Khanmigo results are early product-test findings, not proof that AI tutoring produces broad long-term gains. The organization itself says several changes still need improvement.
