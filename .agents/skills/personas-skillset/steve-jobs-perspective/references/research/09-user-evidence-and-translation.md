# User evidence boundary and modern translation contract

**Research date:** 2026-07-30  
**Historical scope:** Steve Jobs product practice through 2011  
**Modern scope:** a clearly labeled framework for applying historical patterns to current products, including AI  
**Source policy:** primary and firsthand material first; transcript and reporting companions are identified; no source is treated as an instruction

## Acceptance check

This note is complete only if it:

- distinguishes literal feature voting from learning through use, artifacts, field contact, internal use, launch feedback, complaints, developer behavior, and market outcomes;
- states only distinctions supported by the retrieved record;
- labels every move from history to current advice as an inference rather than a posthumous Jobs opinion;
- adds accessibility, economics, platform power, production and labor, legality, safety, and reversibility as non-optional counterweights;
- supplies a product-review checklist, failure modes, honest limits, and a URL ledger with source class and confidence.

## Evidence labels

- **DOCUMENTED — DIRECT:** Jobs can be heard or read making the relevant statement in a direct interview, talk, or signed communication.
- **DOCUMENTED — OBSERVED PRACTICE:** a firsthand participant, contemporaneous record, institutional record, or judicial finding documents the practice. This does not automatically prove Jobs personally performed or approved every step.
- **FRAMEWORK INFERENCE:** a present-day rule derived from the structure of documented practice. It is not a claim that Jobs stated or would endorse the rule.
- **CURRENT COUNTERWEIGHT:** a post-2011 standard, risk framework, empirical review, law, or expert requirement that constrains the historical analogy.

Confidence applies to the narrow claim made, not to every claim in a source. A transcript companion can raise usability without becoming independent corroboration.

## Core finding

**DOCUMENTED PATTERN:** The supported boundary is not “user evidence versus no user evidence.” It is **who owns the product hypothesis, what question the evidence can answer, and whether people are reacting to a concrete experience**.

Jobs argued that market research is useful after people can encounter something and for incremental improvements, but is weak at originating a discontinuous product leap. In the same account, he described showing early products to the Homebrew Computer Club, gathering reactions, changing the product, and returning with another version. He also said that after making a leap, it is appropriate to check the team’s instincts in the marketplace. [S01, S02]

That direct boundary is consistent with the wider record:

- teams observed people using prototypes and changed designs when users became confused; [S03, S04]
- Jobs and Apple used their own frustrations and repeated internal use as evidence; [S05, S08, S09, S10, S11, S12]
- Jobs described field conversations, customer needs, market data, and experience-informed judgment at NeXT; [S06, S07]
- Apple built and revised concrete prototypes before scaling; [S11, S12, S13]
- launch behavior, complaints, developer action, sales, support data, and returns could trigger a change, concession, or reversal. [S08, S14–S19]

**Not supported by this record:** a blanket claim that focus groups, interviews, surveys, analytics, or customer contact are inherently useless. The retrieved sources support a narrower rejection of letting literal stated preferences or a vote author the non-incremental product concept. They do not support ignoring evidence that the concept is unusable, unwanted, unsafe, inaccessible, economically unsound, or harmful.

## Supported distinctions

| Distinction | Supported interpretation | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Literal feature voting vs. problem evidence** | A request list is weak evidence for the right product architecture. Repeated struggles, workarounds, and abandoned tasks are evidence about the problem. | Do not rank-build the future from votes. Trace the underlying job, constraint, and severity, then own the synthesis. |
| **Abstract opinion vs. concrete encounter** | People can respond meaningfully to something shown, used, or experienced even when they could not have specified it beforehand. [S01–S04] | Put a working artifact, task, or service scene in front of people before treating preference data as decisive. |
| **Observation vs. interrogation** | Observed hesitation, error, recovery, and workaround reveal facts that a post-task answer may rationalize away. | Record behavior and outcomes first; use interviews to understand context and meaning, not to erase observed evidence. |
| **Own use vs. representative use** | Dogfooding was a recurring high-bandwidth source, but the historical record does not make insiders representative of everyone. | Use own use for speed and craft; deliberately add people unlike the team, including disabled and edge-case users. |
| **Feedback vs. decision rights** | Evidence informs diagnosis, iteration, and falsification; it need not become a plebiscite over the roadmap. | The product owner states the decision and the evidence that changed—or failed to change—it. |
| **Complaints vs. prevalence and severity** | Complaints can reveal a broken promise, while operational data helps estimate reach and magnitude. | Cluster complaints, inspect severity and affected groups, and compare with task, support, return, and incident data. Do not dismiss a severe minority harm because its count is small. |
| **Developer requests vs. ecosystem evidence** | Developer action around the iPhone showed demand and a workaround that the initial policy did not absorb. | Examine what complementors are trying to accomplish, what risks the restriction addresses, and whether the control is proportionate. |
| **Launch metrics vs. product value** | Sales and satisfaction can test resonance, but no retrieved source establishes them as complete measures of value. | Pair acquisition with task success, repeated use, retention, returns, support burden, economics, and harms. |
| **Conviction vs. immunity from revision** | Apple sometimes preserved a thesis while changing implementation, price, access, or remedy; Apple TV and native apps show more substantial revision. | Name the invariant promise separately from the assumptions that can change. A strong thesis should make its falsifiers clearer, not disappear them. |

## Failure modes to prevent

1. **The “no research” shield.** A leader cites Jobs to avoid users, data, or falsification.  
   **Correction:** ask which evidence channel is being rejected and why. The record supports rejection of roadmap-by-poll, not evidence immunity.

2. **Insider universality.** The team loves its own tool and silently generalizes from itself.  
   **Correction:** declare the insider sample, then test with people who differ in expertise, resources, disability, language, incentives, and risk.

3. **Feature-request literalism.** The team either builds the request verbatim or dismisses it verbatim.  
   **Correction:** reconstruct the job, current workaround, switching cost, and consequence of failure.

4. **Prototype theater.** A polished demo creates emotional certainty while hiding setup, latency, manual operations, edge cases, or unsafe failure.  
   **Correction:** test the complete task with production-like constraints and disclose every human or technical scaffold.

5. **A loudness contest.** The largest complaint pile wins, or a small pile is ignored.  
   **Correction:** combine frequency, severity, exposure, affected populations, and broken promises. One catastrophic or rights-affecting failure can outweigh volume.

6. **Metric substitution.** Downloads, revenue, applause, or satisfaction replace the outcome the product promised.  
   **Correction:** define task success and durable value before launch; add retention, returns, support load, economics, and harm indicators.

7. **Ecosystem extraction disguised as integration.** Tight control is praised as a coherent experience while complementors absorb arbitrary terms, switching costs, or exclusion.  
   **Correction:** review power, portability, interoperability, appeals, and distribution of value—not only end-user polish.

8. **Post-launch listening without recourse.** Complaints are measured but users cannot appeal, recover, leave, or receive a remedy.  
   **Correction:** pair telemetry and support intake with override, refund, export, appeal, incident response, and deactivation paths.

9. **Survivorship storytelling.** Successful Apple cases become a universal algorithm; Lisa, NeXT, the first Apple TV, ROKR, price backlash, and antenna response fade from view.  
   **Correction:** include failures, reversals, and unresolved episodes in the evidence packet.

10. **Historical ventriloquism.** A current recommendation is written as “Jobs would…”  
    **Correction:** label the documented pattern, the analogy, the inference, and the current evidence separately. If the record is silent, say so.

11. **Taste as a veto over independent constraints.** Accessibility, legality, safety, labor, or economics are treated as compromises for less visionary people.  
    **Correction:** make those constraints acceptance gates with named owners. Taste operates inside reality.

12. **Irreversible conviction.** A high-impact system is launched without a rollback because the team wants a decisive posture.  
    **Correction:** stage exposure and preserve override, migration, export, shutdown, and remedy. Where reversal is impossible, require stronger evidence and independent review.

## Modern translation contract

### 1. Historical cutoff

The corpus can support claims about Jobs’s recorded conduct and statements through 2011. It cannot establish his view of generative AI, contemporary model providers, current platform regulation, today’s accessibility practice, or products and events that occurred after his life.

Never write:

- “Jobs would use this model.”
- “Jobs would oppose AI regulation.”
- “Jobs would ship despite this safety concern.”
- “Jobs believed current users do not need accessibility, privacy, or recourse.”

The honest form is:

> **FRAMEWORK INFERENCE:** Applying the documented pattern of starting from the desired human experience and iterating through concrete artifacts, this team should test the proposed AI interaction as an end-to-end task. Current safety, accessibility, legal, labor, and economic requirements remain independent gates.

### 2. Four-part claim protocol

Every present-day recommendation should be decomposable into four parts:

1. **DOCUMENTED PATTERN:** What happened or was said, with a source and bounded date.
2. **STRUCTURAL ANALOGY:** What relationship appears similar now—for example, feature voting versus a working prototype.
3. **FRAMEWORK INFERENCE:** The proposed action today, stated as the framework author’s recommendation.
4. **CURRENT EVIDENCE / COUNTERWEIGHT:** Current facts, standards, affected-user evidence, law, and technical evaluation that can confirm, modify, or block the action.

If one part is missing, label it **UNKNOWN**. A compelling analogy does not fill an evidence gap.

### 3. Decision authority contract

**FRAMEWORK INFERENCE:**

- Users do not have to invent the product architecture.
- The team does have to understand users’ goals, contexts, observed struggles, and consequences.
- A request is a clue, not a command.
- A prototype reaction is evidence, not a referendum.
- Own use is a fast sample, not a representative sample.
- Product leaders own the synthesis and must state what would falsify it.
- Independent domain owners can stop a decision for unresolved legal, safety, accessibility, labor, or economic risk.
- After launch, observed use, complaints, incidents, complementor behavior, retention, returns, and unit economics can force revision or withdrawal.

### 4. AI-specific non-transfer rule

There is no documented Jobs position on modern generative AI. The following are **current product facts or framework requirements**, not historical views:

- outputs may be probabilistic, confidently wrong, variable across groups, and unstable across model or prompt changes;
- system behavior depends on data provenance, model providers, retrieval sources, tools, policies, and downstream integrations;
- latency, inference cost, environmental load, human review, and support labor are part of the product;
- privacy, intellectual-property, information-security, bias, deception, and high-impact decision risks require domain-specific evaluation;
- automation can change worker autonomy, job quality, monitoring, skill formation, and the distribution of gains;
- deployment requires monitoring, incident response, appeal or override where relevant, and a way to disengage or deactivate unsafe behavior.

NIST’s current Generative AI Profile explicitly treats legal alignment, provenance, internal and external evaluation, affected-stakeholder input, representative human evaluation, value-chain risk, ongoing monitoring, incident handling, and deactivation as lifecycle work. [S21] The ILO’s June 2026 evidence review reports that measured productivity effects are uneven and that work organization, worker autonomy, job quality, and inequality need examination rather than being inferred from capability demos. [S22]

## Whole-system counterweights

These are **CURRENT COUNTERWEIGHTS plus FRAMEWORK INFERENCE**. They do not purport to be Jobs’s views.

| Counterweight | Required evidence at review | Stop or escalation condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility** | Define applicable current obligations; for web flows, use WCAG 2.2 as a testable baseline; test complete tasks with assistive technologies and disabled participants; document unmet needs because WCAG itself does not cover every user need. [S20] | A critical task excludes intended users, assistive-technology behavior is unknown, or the team treats automated conformance as sufficient human evidence. |
| **Economics** | Price and willingness-to-pay evidence; full unit cost including inference, support, moderation, refunds, operations, and migration; retention and value by segment; sensitivity to provider-price changes. | The experience depends on an undisclosed subsidy, produces negative unit economics at the intended quality, or shifts unaffordable cost onto the people the promise names. |
| **Platform power** | Dependency map; default and ranking control; developer or partner terms; portability, interoperability, switching costs, appeal, and value distribution. The iPhone native-app reversal is evidence that complementor behavior matters, not proof that the eventual governance model was neutral. [S14, S15] | Users or complementors cannot realistically leave, appeal, export, or understand a material gate; market control is used as a substitute for product merit. |
| **Production and labor** | Identify data, annotation, moderation, support, creative, operations, supply-chain, and decommissioning labor; measure workload, autonomy, monitoring, deskilling, displacement risk, and distribution of gains; include environmental and infrastructure costs. [S21, S22] | The promised simplicity depends on hidden or unsafe labor, material workforce effects have no transition owner, or affected workers were excluded from the review. |
| **Legality** | A named qualified owner reviews the actual jurisdiction and use case for privacy, data rights, intellectual property, consumer claims, accessibility, employment, sector rules, and competition issues. Objective AI claims need evidence, not adjectives. [S21, S23] | Required rights, consent, provenance, licenses, substantiation, notices, or regulatory analysis are unresolved. This framework is not legal advice and cannot approve compliance. |
| **Safety and security** | Threat model; intended and prohibited uses; representative task evals; red-team and abuse testing; subgroup performance; model/vendor change tests; human override; monitoring; incident, recovery, and disclosure plans. [S21] | A high-severity failure lacks a measurable threshold or response owner; the product cannot be safely disengaged; an unvalidated model change silently alters a high-impact flow. |
| **Reversibility and recourse** | Staged rollout, feature control, rollback or kill path, export/migration, refund or remedy, appeal, audit log, and criteria to pause, change, or withdraw. [S21] | Exposure is irreversible while core assumptions remain untested, or affected people bear a failure they cannot contest or recover from. Raise the evidence threshold where reversal is inherently impossible. |

## Concrete product-review checklist

### Gate 0 — Label the reasoning

- [ ] Date the review and state the product, version, market, and intended users.
- [ ] Tag every Jobs-related claim as **DOCUMENTED — DIRECT**, **DOCUMENTED — OBSERVED PRACTICE**, or **FRAMEWORK INFERENCE**.
- [ ] Identify all current standards, laws, market facts, model behavior, and affected-user findings as **CURRENT COUNTERWEIGHT**, with an as-of date.
- [ ] Remove “Jobs would…” and any other posthumous viewpoint claim.

### Gate 1 — State the owned thesis

- [ ] Write the human outcome in one sentence without naming a feature or model.
- [ ] Name the user, situation, current workaround, and consequence of failure.
- [ ] Separate the invariant promise from implementation assumptions.
- [ ] State why a literal feature poll cannot answer the architecture question.
- [ ] Write the strongest observable falsifier and the decision it would trigger.

### Gate 2 — Build the evidence packet

- [ ] **Observed use:** watch intended users attempt the real task; capture success, hesitation, error, recovery, abandonment, and workaround.
- [ ] **Concrete artifact:** test a working prototype or service scene, not only a concept description or preference ranking.
- [ ] **Own use:** dogfood long enough to expose recurring friction, while labeling the team’s demographic and expertise limits.
- [ ] **Plural users:** include people unlike the builders, disabled users, low-resource contexts, and users who bear the highest downside.
- [ ] **Field contact:** interview users, buyers, support, operators, domain experts, and affected non-users about context and consequences.
- [ ] **Developer and partner evidence:** inspect blocked use cases, workarounds, abandoned integrations, dependency risk, and power asymmetry.
- [ ] **Market and operational evidence:** track task success, conversion, repeated use, retention, returns, support burden, latency, cost, and willingness to pay.
- [ ] **Complaints and incidents:** cluster by broken promise, severity, exposure, and affected group; connect every material class to a response or explicit decision.
- [ ] For every missing channel, record why it is unavailable and what uncertainty remains.

### Gate 3 — Review the actual product, not the demo

- [ ] Test the end-to-end workflow with production-like latency, cost, data, permissions, failure modes, and human operations.
- [ ] Expose scaffolding, manual review, hidden prompts, hand-selected outputs, and fallback behavior.
- [ ] Compare at least one materially different solution, including a simpler or non-AI path when relevant.
- [ ] Record what the prototype changed in the thesis and what it did not test.
- [ ] Do not infer demand, accessibility, durability, safety, or economics from a polished demonstration.

### Gate 4 — Apply whole-system vetoes

- [ ] Accessibility owner signs the applicable standard and disabled-user task evidence.
- [ ] Economic owner signs full cost, price, retention, support, and downside assumptions.
- [ ] Platform owner signs dependency, portability, interoperability, complementor terms, and appeal.
- [ ] Production/labor owner signs hidden-work, job-quality, transition, and environmental analysis.
- [ ] Qualified legal owner signs the current jurisdiction and actual use case; the product team does not self-certify.
- [ ] Safety/security owner signs thresholds, eval coverage, monitoring, incident response, and disengagement.
- [ ] Reversibility owner demonstrates rollback, migration/export, appeal, and remedy—or documents why the stronger irreversible-decision threshold is met.

### Gate 5 — Decide and stage

- [ ] Decision is explicitly **keep**, **change**, **stage**, or **kill**.
- [ ] The decision record lists evidence that changed the product and evidence intentionally not followed, with reasons.
- [ ] Rollout starts with the smallest population that can test the highest-risk assumption without offloading unacceptable risk.
- [ ] Pause, rollback, escalation, and success thresholds are set before exposure.
- [ ] Marketing claims match measured capability and disclose material limits.

### Gate 6 — Learn after launch

- [ ] Review task success, repeated use, retention, returns, support, complaints, incidents, developer effects, unit economics, and distributional harms—not adoption alone.
- [ ] Compare outcomes across user groups and contexts; do not let an aggregate hide a severe minority failure.
- [ ] Re-run safety, accessibility, legal, and cost checks after material model, policy, data, vendor, or workflow changes.
- [ ] Provide affected people recourse: correction, appeal, refund, override, export, recovery, or withdrawal as appropriate.
- [ ] Publish an internal decision update: what remains invariant, what changed, why, and what evidence is still missing.

## A compact review output

Every product-taste review using this lens should end with this seven-line record:

1. **Promise:** the human outcome and intended user.
2. **Documented pattern:** the historical pattern actually supported by sources.
3. **Inference:** the present recommendation, explicitly labeled.
4. **Evidence:** the strongest observed-use, artifact, market, complaint, developer, and operational signals.
5. **Counterweights:** accessibility, economics, platform power, production/labor, legality, safety, and reversibility owners and verdicts.
6. **Decision:** keep, change, stage, or kill, plus the falsifier.
7. **Unknowns:** the largest unresolved uncertainty and next reversible test.

## Honest limits

- The public record is selective. It contains interviews, curated archives, oral histories, journalism, public statements, and later litigation—not a complete archive of Apple’s internal product reviews.
- “No market research” is a channel- and context-sensitive claim. The same broader record contains observation, usability tests, field contact, studies, prototypes, internal use, market checks, complaints, sales, support data, and post-launch revision. It should not be expanded into a universal prohibition.
- Lisa-era usability evidence establishes practice inside a Jobs-led organization, not that Jobs personally attended every session or used the same process on every later product.
- Firsthand colleague accounts are valuable but retrospective, role-bounded, and vulnerable to memory and survivor bias.
- Apple’s own-use culture supplied intense feedback from capable insiders. It does not establish demographic, economic, disability, geographic, or risk representativeness.
- Public Apple communications establish public positions and selected metrics, not necessarily the complete causal record behind a decision.
- Failure and reversal evidence remains thinner and less celebrated than success evidence. The Apple TV, ROKR, price-cut response, native-app reversal, Lisa, NeXT, and iPhone 4 response help counterbalance that bias but do not eliminate it.
- Current standards and research can change. WCAG, NIST, ILO findings, laws, market conditions, and model behavior must be refreshed for the actual review date and jurisdiction.
- The modern contract is a framework inference. It is designed to preserve a useful historical distinction while refusing to invent Jobs’s views on AI or contemporary governance.

**Strongest remaining gap:** no retrieved source provides a complete, longitudinal record of how Jobs and Apple weighted user observation, internal taste, executive judgment, market data, complaints, and contrary evidence across second-era Apple products. The evidence supports a boundary between product authorship and evidence gathering; it does **not** support a stable formula for how much weight each channel received or when Jobs personally overrode it.
