# Martin Fowler: documented decisions and action evidence

**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-09  
**Scope:** ten consequential, publicly documented choices or turning points. This is decision evidence for later synthesis, not a biography and not a finished persona skill.  
**Evidence base:** 29 legally public source pages: 28 primary or co-primary records and one official publisher record. Primary/co-primary share: **97%**. No private motive is inferred.

## Evidence conventions

- **Primary:** Fowler's own retrospective, essay, diary, or contemporaneous statement.
- **Co-primary:** a record Fowler co-authored or an official record of a collective action in which he participated.
- **Publisher record:** an official bibliographic/product record; reliable for publication facts, not inner reasoning.
- **Inference:** the analyst's comparison between action and stated principles. It is not represented as Fowler's own conclusion.
- **Confidence:** **High** means the action and reasoning are explicit in primary records; **Medium** means the action is clear but motives, causality, or outcomes are partly retrospective; **Low** means evidence is too thin for a firm behavioral inference.

The cases are selected for an observable commitment: changing employment, joining a project or collective statement, writing or rejecting a book, changing an editorial platform, imposing production constraints, publishing a corrective to one's earlier influence, stopping a major career activity, or accepting a new organizational role. Generic opinions were excluded.

## Decision cases

### 1. Breaking an independence rule to join Thoughtworks (1991–2000)

**Context and constraint.** Fowler became an independent consultant in 1991. He says he had not imagined joining a client, partly because independence protected the writing central to his life and partly because he had not found a company he wanted to work for. He began consulting with Thoughtworks in spring 1999 on a troubled leasing project; the engagement became unusually long. **[High | Primary | [About Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/aboutMe.html), undated living page; [Thoughtworks journey transcript](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/podcasts/technology-podcasts/martin-fowler-my-thoughtworks-journey), 2019-12-27]**

**Decision.** When Roy Singham invited him to join full-time, Fowler reversed his prior “never” rule and became an employee in 2000. **[High | Primary retrospective | same sources]**

**Stated reasoning.** Thoughtworks differed from clients that treated obstacles as reasons agile ideas could not work: its response was to ask how to tackle the obstacles. He also says its treatment of people and customers fit his views, especially its non-clichéd belief that people were its greatest asset. **[High | Primary retrospective | same sources]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** What he initially framed as perhaps a couple of years had lasted twenty years by the 2019 interview and continues on his current disclosure page. His role remained unusually stable: writing, communicating field experience, and advising rather than climbing a conventional role ladder. He also discloses salary and equity, making the institutional relationship and potential bias visible. **[High | Primary | same sources]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** the action conflicts with his prior employment rule but aligns with the deeper reasons for that rule—editorial independence, people-centered delivery, and space to write. It suggests a hierarchy: preserve the purpose, not the literal rule. The evidence does not establish why he first became independent beyond the facts he states. **[High for the conflict; Medium for the hierarchical inference]**

### 2. Letting C3 experience revise his view of Extreme Programming (1993–1999)

**Context and constraint.** Fowler consulted on Chrysler's C3 payroll effort from 1993, part-time through late 1996. The project was restarted under Kent Beck in 1996 after earlier instability. Fowler later wrote that there was a large difference between how he expected XP to work and what he saw on C3. **[High | Primary | [C3](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/C3.html), 2004-08-03; [Variations on a Theme of XP](https://martinfowler.com/articles/xpVariation.html), 2001-01]**

**Decision.** He participated in the rebooted project, learned the practices as an interacting whole, then helped carry the experience outward through consulting, essays, conference work, and the co-authored *Planning Extreme Programming*. **[High | Primary | [Planning Extreme Programming](https://martinfowler.com/books/pxp.html), 2000; [The New Methodology](https://martinfowler.com/articles/newMethodology.html), original 2000-07, revised 2005-12-13]**

**Stated reasoning.** Fowler emphasizes that the interaction among XP practices is hard to understand by reading and speculation alone; trying the system “by the book” precedes informed adaptation. He singled out its testing foundation and the simplicity of its planning practice. **[High | Primary | [Variations on a Theme of XP](https://martinfowler.com/articles/xpVariation.html), 2001-01; [Planning XP](https://martinfowler.com/books/pxp.html), 2000]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** C3 went live in 1997 paying roughly ten thousand people, but new development stopped in 1999 and the live system was later largely replaced. Fowler explicitly preserves both sides: the early success helped launch XP, while cancellation proves XP is no guarantee of project success. He also declines to offer a full causal account because he was only part-time and left before the later history. **[High | Primary retrospective | [C3](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/C3.html), 2004-08-03]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** the case strongly aligns with an experience-before-abstraction principle and with scope-limited testimony. The unresolved tension—celebrated birth project and eventual cancellation—is evidence he does not sanitize an origin story. **[High]**

### 3. Helping turn a loose methods meeting into the Agile Manifesto (2000–2001)

**Context and constraint.** A 2000 XP retreat exposed common ground among several “lightweight” methods while retaining XP as a specific “stake in the ground.” Fowler went to Snowbird in February 2001 with deliberately limited expectations: useful relationships, not necessarily a joint declaration. **[High | Primary retrospective | [Writing the Agile Manifesto](https://martinfowler.com/articles/agileStory.html), 2006-07-09]**

**Decision.** Fowler joined the seventeen participants in choosing the word `agile`, drafting values and later principles, and publishing a rallying document rather than merely exchanging views. The official record lists him among the authors. **[High | Primary/co-primary | [Manifesto for Agile Software Development](https://agilemanifesto.org/), 2001-02; [Writing the Agile Manifesto](https://martinfowler.com/articles/agileStory.html), 2006-07-09]**

**Stated reasoning.** Once the group discovered substantial agreement, they wanted both to capture it and distinguish it clearly from dominant industry practice. Fowler thought the values had more punch than the subsequently completed principles. **[High | Primary retrospective | [Writing the Agile Manifesto](https://martinfowler.com/articles/agileStory.html), 2006-07-09]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** The four values and twelve principles remain the official public artifact. Fowler later stressed that the seventeen had no unique authority over agile's future; the Agile Alliance became a separate nonprofit rather than a permanent council of founders. **[High | Co-primary and Primary | [Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/), 2001-02; [Fowler retrospective](https://martinfowler.com/articles/agileStory.html), 2006-07-09]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** the act aligns with concise shared vocabulary and coalition-building, while his refusal of founder privilege resists turning a useful text into personal authority. The manifesto's fame nonetheless gave its authors durable status—a consequence that sits uneasily beside that refusal. **[High for stated refusal; Medium for the social tension]**

### 4. Converting C3's tacit refactoring practice into a teachable book (1996–1999)

**Context and constraint.** Fowler learned from Beck's continual reworking of the C3 codebase. In later consulting he wanted to recommend the technique but could not point people to a book that taught it. **[High | Primary | [Second-edition diary](https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-ed.html), 2018; [Refactoring first-edition record and sample](https://www.informit.com/store/refactoring-improving-the-design-of-existing-code-9780201485677), published 1999-06-28]**

**Decision.** He wrote *Refactoring* rather than leaving the knowledge as expert lore. The package combined a whole-process example, principles, smells, testing, and a catalog whose entries contained motivation, mechanics, and a small example. He credited Beck and many other contributors rather than presenting the technique as his invention. **[High | Primary plus publisher record | [Refactoring book page](https://martinfowler.com/books/refactoring.html), undated living page; [InformIT](https://www.informit.com/store/refactoring-improving-the-design-of-existing-code-9780201485677), 1999-06-28]**

**Stated reasoning.** The immediate gap was pedagogical: a valuable practice existed but lacked an accessible learning vehicle. The small-step form was itself part of the argument, lowering the risk of behavior-changing errors. **[High | Primary | [Second-edition diary](https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-ed.html), 2018; [book page](https://martinfowler.com/books/refactoring.html), undated]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** Addison-Wesley published the 464-page first edition in 1999. Fowler later called it the book he was proudest of, noting IDE automation and the term's entry into the software lexicon—while also lamenting its misuse. Those impact judgments are Fowler's own retrospective, not an independent impact study. **[High for publication; Medium for impact magnitude | Publisher record and Primary | same sources]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** this is the clearest action-level instance of his self-described role as a recognizer and packager of others' ideas. The tension is productive: the package made distributed practice legible, but authorial visibility can obscure its collective origin, which his acknowledgments repeatedly restore. **[High]**

### 5. Governing the Signature Series strictly enough to reject his own book (2002–2018)

**Context and constraint.** Addison-Wesley launched Fowler's Signature Series in 2002. Fowler wanted experience-accelerating, durable technical books for enterprise technical leaders—not a general imprint or a series driven by volume. **[High | Primary | [My Books / Signature Series](https://martinfowler.com/books/), undated living page; [Signature Series Criteria](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/SignatureSeriesCriteria.html), 2004-08-03]**

**Decision.** He required a substantial draft before acceptance, asked existing series authors to review candidates, retained final judgment, and preferred rejecting a worthy book over admitting a sub-par one. The process applied to him: after peer input, he excluded his own *NoSQL Distilled* because it did not fit the series, while later admitting *Refactoring* 2e. **[High | Primary | [Signature Series Criteria](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/SignatureSeriesCriteria.html), 2004-08-03; [second-edition diary, “Refactoring takes the Red and Black”](https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-ed.html), 2018-04-05 memo]**

**Stated reasoning.** The series brand should let a busy reader trust the books' quality and relevance. Peer authors served partly as a check against his being too generous to books he personally liked. **[High | Primary | [Signature Series Criteria](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/SignatureSeriesCriteria.html), 2004-08-03]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** The current catalog shows a deliberately small collection and reports seven Jolt awards. The awards claim comes from Fowler's catalog, not an independently audited ledger here. His self-rejection is a direct process outcome; *Refactoring* 2e's later inclusion shows “my book” was neither an automatic yes nor an automatic no. **[High for decisions; Medium for award/result summary | Primary | [books page](https://martinfowler.com/books/), undated]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** strong alignment with quality-over-quantity, external review, and criteria applied symmetrically despite ego or sunk effort. There is also a structural tension: he designed the criteria and retained final authority even while using peers to constrain himself. **[High]**

### 6. Turning martinfowler.com from a personal archive into a selective publishing platform (1999–present)

**Context and constraint.** Fowler obtained the domain around 1999, initially posting copies of articles and talk notes. He disliked blogs' temporal emphasis and did not want hard-won writing to vanish with the news cycle. **[High | Primary retrospective | [Thoughtworks journey](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/podcasts/technology-podcasts/martin-fowler-my-thoughtworks-journey), 2019-12-27; [What Is a Bliki?](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/WhatIsaBliki.html), 2003-05-26]**

**Decision.** In 2003 he built a cross between blog and wiki, intended as a lasting cross-linked body of short material. As readership grew, he deliberately opened the platform to other writers, vetting contributions and often doing developmental editing, while keeping editorial independence and flexible web presentation. **[High | Primary | [What Is a Bliki?](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/WhatIsaBliki.html), 2003-05-26; [About](https://martinfowler.com/aboutMe.html), undated; [Thoughtworks journey](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/podcasts/technology-podcasts/martin-fowler-my-thoughtworks-journey), 2019-12-27]**

**Stated reasoning.** The bliki joined short-form immediacy to durable retrieval. The later platform gave good writers a “signal boost”; low publication frequency was acceptable because quality mattered more than quantity. Organizational distance from Thoughtworks' corporate surface also made outside contribution easier. **[High | Primary | same sources]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** By 2019 Fowler said most strong recent articles on the site were written by others and described his role as developmental editor. He regretted that the personal domain implied sole authorship, but valued what it had become. A 2014 site report records nine contributor-involved articles on its evergreen list; these are self-reported analytics, useful but not independently verified. **[High for role change; Medium for analytics | Primary | [2014 site report](https://martinfowler.com/articles/2014-site-report/), published/circulated 2015-01]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** this aligns with packaging and attribution, and replaces personal output as the bottleneck with editorial leverage. The durable tension is personal-brand reach versus collective authorship; he acknowledges it without renaming the platform. **[High]**

### 7. Rebuilding *Refactoring* as reader-first, web-first, and size-constrained (2015–2018)

**Context and constraint.** The first edition still taught the practice but had dated Java idioms and a class-centered frame. Fowler was reluctant because second editions often fail to improve on originals. Exploratory essays in 2015–2016 helped him decide to begin a two-year rewrite. **[High | Primary production diary | [The Second Edition of Refactoring](https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-ed.html), 2018]**

**Decision.** He used JavaScript despite disliking parts of the language, chose a single example language rather than several, made the web edition canonical, held the topic scope roughly constant, and capped the physical book at the first edition's 412 content pages. When proofs reached 440 pages, he prioritized entries, removed five lower-priority refactorings and a redundant example from print, and reached 410 pages; removed material remained in the web edition. **[High | Primary production diary | same source; [web-edition access note](https://martinfowler.com/articles/access-refactoring-web-edition.html), 2018-11-21]**

**Stated reasoning.** JavaScript was more broadly readable, had familiar syntax, supported classes but was not wholly class-centered, and made functions first-class. One language reduced reader switching cost. The web could hold an evolving integrated work, while the page cap resisted second-edition bloat; he explicitly judged copyedits and examples from the reader's perspective. **[High | Primary | [second-edition diary](https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-ed.html), 2018]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** The 2018 edition shipped with a canonical web edition containing material absent from print. Fowler predicted many existing readers would dislike changes because of loss aversion, but said the real test was whether future newcomers could learn from it over five, ten, or twenty years—an outcome he said could not yet be assessed. **[High | Primary | [second-edition diary](https://martinfowler.com/articles/refactoring-2nd-ed.html), 2018; [access note](https://martinfowler.com/articles/access-refactoring-web-edition.html), 2018-11-21]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** this aligns with designing for readers, limiting scope, testing through staged review, and treating format as mutable representation. It conflicts with personal language taste and publishing-industry ebook conventions; Fowler chose reader accessibility over the former but could not fully override the latter. **[High]**

### 8. Correcting the enthusiasm catalyzed by the microservices article (2013–2020)

**Context and constraint.** Concerned that `microservices` lacked a stable meaning, Fowler teamed with experienced practitioner James Lewis to describe field-observed characteristics in March 2014. Fowler's own site report says the article rapidly became the site's most-read article and likely had a catalytic effect on interest, while disclaiming sole credit or blame. **[High for action; Medium for causal impact | Co-primary and Primary self-report | [Microservices](https://martinfowler.com/articles/microservices.html), 2014-03-25; [2014 site report](https://martinfowler.com/articles/2014-site-report/), 2015-01]**

**Decision.** Rather than let the definition stand as advocacy, he quickly published prerequisites and uncertainty, then in 2015 named the `Microservice Premium` and advanced the tentative `Monolith First` heuristic. He also published a contrary guest response on his own site. **[High | Primary editorial record | [Microservices and the First Law of Distributed Objects](https://martinfowler.com/articles/distributed-objects-microservices.html), 2014-08-13; [Microservice Premium](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/MicroservicePremium.html), 2015-05-13; [Monolith First](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/MonolithFirst.html), 2015-06-03; [Microservices Guide](https://martinfowler.com/microservices/), 2019-08-21]**

**Stated reasoning.** Distribution, consistency, and operational complexity impose real cost and risk. Successful stories he heard tended to begin with monoliths, while greenfield microservice stories often went badly—but he explicitly said his anecdotal evidence was insufficient for a firm universal rule. In 2014 he stated the writer's duty was to communicate contradictory lessons clearly so readers could decide. **[High | Primary | same sources]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** By the 2019 guide his summary was blunt: microservices are useful, but many or most situations are better served by a monolith. The guide preserves Stefan Tilkov's direct disagreement and a 2020 interview in which Fowler remains skeptical of some claimed benefits. **[High | Primary/editorial record | [Microservices Guide](https://martinfowler.com/microservices/), 2019-08-21, with 2020 interview entry]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** the corrective aligns with making trade-offs explicit and revising public guidance as adverse reports accumulate. The tension is genuine: his platform helped amplify a fashion that later required warnings. He does not deny that catalytic role, but the evidence does not show a reversal from “microservices are good” to “bad”; the original article was descriptive and already qualified. **[High]**

### 9. Retiring from a successful speaking career and redirecting effort to editorial work (2021)

**Context and constraint.** Speaking was a career pillar and a valuable part of Fowler's public role at Thoughtworks. Yet he describes worsening anticipatory anxiety, disrupted sleep, physical distress, travel costs, and a longstanding effort to avoid engagements. The COVID pause demonstrated the value of not speaking. **[High | Primary | [Retiring from Speaking](https://martinfowler.com/articles/202106-reducing-speaking.html), 2021-06-29]**

**Decision.** He publicly committed to refuse almost every future talk request and set a firm boundary with organizational support. **[High | Primary | same source]**

**Stated reasoning.** At that stage he could avoid work that made him miserable; good substitute speakers existed; and his saved time and energy could produce more value through writing and developmental editing, especially as he became less connected to day-to-day delivery. **[High | Primary | same source]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** His current biography describes him as retired from speaking. Later appearances do exist, including conversations rather than a full return to the old keynote circuit, so “almost every request” was a boundary, not a literal never. **[High for retirement statement; Medium for interpreting later exceptions | Primary | [About](https://martinfowler.com/aboutMe.html), undated living page; [Retiring](https://martinfowler.com/articles/202106-reducing-speaking.html), 2021-06-29]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** this aligns with explicit constraints, comparative advantage, and handing visibility to others. It conflicts with professional pride and a public-facing organizational role; the delayed decision also shows he tolerated a personally costly channel for years before changing it. **[High]**

### 10. Moving from AI observer to coordinator and selective user—without delegating responsibility (2023–2026)

**Context and constraint.** Fowler's generative-AI hub initially described him as intrigued but unsure and records that he took a Thoughtworks role coordinating investigation of AI's effects on delivery. In June 2025 he called LLMs a change comparable to high-level languages while admitting he had only dabbled with the best tools. **[High | Primary | [Exploring Generative AI](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai.html), undated living collection with entries from 2023; [LLMs bring a new nature of abstraction](https://martinfowler.com/articles/2025-nature-abstraction.html), 2025-06-24]**

**Decision.** He chose an experiment-and-publish posture: coordinate colleagues' empirical reports, urge readers to compare detailed workflows rather than aggregate “AI productivity,” and personally adopt bounded uses. By April–May 2026 he reported using Gemini for retrieval and trying an `Interrogatory LLM` to elicit context; he also formalized a distinction between reviewed `agentic programming` and code-ignoring `vibe coding`. **[High | Primary | [Some thoughts on LLMs and Software Development](https://martinfowler.com/articles/202508-ai-thoughts.html), 2025-08-28; [Fragments: April 29](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-04-29.html), 2026-04-29; [Interrogatory LLM](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/InterrogatoryLLM.html), 2026-05-14; [Agentic Programming](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/AgenticProgramming.html), 2026-05-21]**

**Stated reasoning.** Workflows and model capability materially change results, so broad surveys may mislead. He recommends experimentation but says he does not know the profession's future. In agentic programming, humans remain responsible and need review, tests, and other sensors. For prose, he draws a sharper boundary: writing is part of his thinking, so having an LLM write his articles would damage the understanding he wants to develop. **[High | Primary | [AI thoughts](https://martinfowler.com/articles/202508-ai-thoughts.html), 2025-08-28; [Fragments: April 29](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-04-29.html), 2026-04-29; [Agentic Programming](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/AgenticProgramming.html), 2026-05-21]**

**Observable result and later reflection.** In May 2026 he identified November 2025 as a capability inflection for agentic programming, but did so mainly through others' documented experiences. He had moved from “dabbled” to named personal uses, not to a public claim of fully agent-written software. His published distinctions preserve maintainability, security, domain understanding, and human responsibility as constraints on adoption. **[High for chronology and stated limits; Medium for degree of personal adoption | Primary | [November Inflection](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/NovemberInflection.html), 2026-05-01; [Interrogatory LLM](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/InterrogatoryLLM.html), 2026-05-14; [Agentic Programming](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/AgenticProgramming.html), 2026-05-21]**

**Alignment/tension.** **Inference:** this aligns with learning through concrete reports, separating terms by consequences, and retaining human feedback loops. The tension is deliberate: he predicts profound change and adopts useful tools while rejecting both confident forecasts and outsourcing the writing through which he thinks. **[High]**

## Cross-case decision patterns

These are inferences from the cases, not direct Fowler maxims.

1. **Preserve purpose over prior rule.** Joining Thoughtworks broke an employment rule but preserved its purpose; JavaScript violated taste but served readers; later speaking limits protected the writing/editorial work he valued more. **[High | Cases 1, 7, 9]**
2. **Act after contact with working practice.** C3 experience preceded XP advocacy and refactoring packaging; microservices guidance came from practitioner reports; the AI program foregrounds detailed workflows rather than undifferentiated surveys. **[High | Cases 2, 4, 8, 10]**
3. **Externalize quality control.** Signature authors review candidates; book reviewers see chapters and full drafts; site contributors receive developmental editing; agentic work needs tests, review, and sensors. **[High | Cases 5–7, 10]**
4. **Use reversible or bounded experiments before large commitments.** Exploratory refactoring essays preceded the rewrite; the bliki allowed immature ideas to evolve; AI experimentation remains use-specific. C3/XP is the partial counterexample: he argues trying the integrated practice faithfully may be necessary before adapting it. **[Medium-High | Cases 2, 6, 7, 10]**
5. **Publish the countercase, including against himself.** He records C3's cancellation, rejects his own book, hosts an anti-`Monolith First` response, predicts disappointment with his second edition, and states uncertainty on AI. **[High | Cases 2, 5, 7, 8, 10]**
6. **Convert personal reach into other people's leverage.** The Signature Series, site platform, speaking retirement, and AI research hub increasingly shift him from sole author/speaker toward curator, editor, and amplifier. **[High | Cases 5, 6, 9, 10]**

## Contradictions and unresolved limits

- **Independence versus institution:** Thoughtworks protects and hosts much of Fowler's work, but it also pays him and he holds equity. Editorial distance reduces but does not erase institutional incentive. Fowler discloses this; the public record cannot measure its effect. **[High | Primary fact; unknown causal effect]**
- **Experience-first versus public influence:** his writing often formalizes field practice, yet the resulting label can accelerate adoption faster than the evidence base matures—as microservices illustrates. **[High | Cases 4 and 8]**
- **Quality gate versus personal authority:** peer panels and reviewers constrain Fowler, but he retains final series and site decisions. This is accountable curation, not democratic neutrality. **[High]**
- **Evolving web artifact versus fixed commercial product:** the canonical web edition can grow, while purchaser access, ebook parity, and physical production remain publisher-constrained. **[High | Case 7]**
- **Agile founder versus refusal of founder status:** he helped write a durable authority-bearing document while arguing its authors should have no special authority over the movement. Both statements are documented; the cultural contradiction cannot be resolved by intent alone. **[Medium-High]**
- **AI transformation versus personal restraint:** “profound change” does not equal total adoption. The evidence supports coordination, retrieval use, an elicitation experiment, and conceptual framing; it does **not** support claiming that Fowler now delegates most coding or writing to agents. **[High]**

## Candidate decision heuristics for later synthesis

1. If a rule's literal form blocks its purpose, preserve the purpose and revise the rule.
2. Before recommending an integrated practice, observe it in real work; before adapting it, learn how its parts interact.
3. When useful tacit knowledge lacks a teaching vehicle, package it as definition, worked example, mechanics, and catalog.
4. Put ego-sensitive decisions through a peer mechanism strong enough to say no.
5. Give a public label boundaries and prerequisites; if the label becomes hype, publish the costs and countercases prominently.
6. For a revision, state hard constraints early—audience, scope, size, canonical form—and cut by dependency and priority.
7. Treat personal reach as a scarce resource that can amplify better-situated practitioners.
8. When a successful activity has high recurring personal cost, set a boundary and redirect the capacity toward comparative advantage.
9. For a fast-changing tool, separate workflow classes, collect detailed field reports, experiment narrowly, and keep responsibility with the human.
10. State which part of a result is observed, self-reported, retrospective, or still unknowable.
